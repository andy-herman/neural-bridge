## Key question 1: Which firms must appoint a CISO today, what changed under the 2026 Network Act amendment, when, and what is still only proposed?

### Takeaway
The amended Information and Communications Network Act (정보통신망 이용촉진 및 정보보호 등에 관한 법률, "Network Act") passed the National Assembly on 2026-03-12 and, together with its Enforcement Decree, took effect on 2026-10-01 per MSIT: CISOs at all but "medium-sized" ICT service providers must now be executives (임원), the CISO's statutory duties now include staffing, budgeting and board reporting, large providers must run an Information Security Committee chaired by the CISO that reports to the CEO and board, and a penalty surcharge of up to 3% of related revenue applies to repeat breaches caused by intent or gross negligence. The pre-existing no-concurrent-role rule (assets ≥ KRW 5 trillion, or ISMS-mandatory with assets ≥ KRW 500 billion) remains the dedicated-CISO threshold. Still pending: CEO-as-ultimate-responsible-person provisions in the PIPA and EFTA bills, burden-of-proof reversal and punitive damages (Lee Hae-min bill), and the national cybersecurity basic act.

### Inferences
- The "executive CISO" rule plus statutory board-reporting duty converts the CISO from a designated manager into a governance officer in law; combined with the committee requirement it gives a CISO a legally grounded channel to the board that did not previously exist outside the financial sector.
- Because the dedicated-CISO thresholds (KRW 5 trillion / KRW 500 billion + ISMS) were not changed by the 2026 amendment as reported, most mid-cap companies will now have an executive CISO who may still hold other roles; the "no other role" rule remains a large-company rule.
- The 3% surcharge and ex officio investigation powers mirror the PIPC's toolkit, so a single large hack now carries parallel MSIT (Network Act) and PIPC (PIPA) surcharge exposure.

### Gaps
- Exact Enforcement Decree thresholds for the Information Security Committee (Art. 45-4) and for the "medium-sized enterprise" carve-out were not found in any retrieved extract; the primary decree text on law.go.kr could not be fetched.
- Whether the 2026 amendment changed the ex-ante CISO reporting (신고) obligation scope could not be confirmed.
- The law.go.kr "Act No. 21988 / effective 2026-10-02" entry is unresolved against the MSIT 2026-10-01 date.

## Key question 2: How many Korean firms have a CISO who also holds another title, and what does Information Security Disclosure data say about security spend and staff?

### Takeaway
KISA disclosure-portal data cited by Boan News shows that in 2024, 78% of the 773 disclosing companies had a CISO holding concurrent duties and about 29% of CISOs were not executives. MSIT's 2025 disclosure analysis (773 companies, FY2024 data) reports total security investment of KRW 2.423 trillion (+14.3%), an average of KRW 3.2 billion per company, 8,506 dedicated security staff (+10.7%) and an average of 11.2 staff per company; the most recent explicit ratio found is roughly 9% to 10% of IT investment and staff, with a 9.13% average published in the 2022 analysis.

## Key question 3: What enforcement has happened under the stronger PIPA penalties, and what do commentators say about deterrence?

### Takeaway
Under the 3%-of-total-revenue regime in force since 2023, the PIPC levied KRW 134.79 billion on SK Telecom (2025-08-28), roughly KRW 54 billion on KT (2026), and a record KRW 624.7 billion on Coupang (2026-06-11; KRW 423.6 billion for the breach, KRW 201.1 billion for unlawful tracking), all of which are being or will be contested in the Seoul Administrative Court. A new 10% ceiling for intentional or grossly negligent repeat violations, mega-breaches of 10 million or more people, or breaches following an unmet corrective order took effect 2026-09-11. Security practitioners called the Coupang fine proportionate and a wake-up call, while academics and business groups argued revenue-based fines are disproportionate and inconsistently applied; SK Telecom's KRW 1.2 trillion remediation spend and the carriers' lighter fines are the main deterrence counterarguments.

## Key question 4: Which bills are pending in the National Assembly as of October 2026, who sponsors them, and what do they do? (Also: breach notification timelines, EFTA and the 5-5-7 rule, CIIP Act, national cybersecurity basic act, and reforms after the 2025-2026 incident wave)

### Takeaway
Passed and in force: the Network Act package (2026-10-01), the PIPA 10% surcharge (2026-09-11), the Feb 2025 principle-based rewrite of the Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulation (which deleted the 5-5-7 numbers from Art. 8(2)), and mandatory board reporting by financial firms' information security committees (2025-08-05). Pending: Rep. Yoo Dong-soo's EFTA bill (CEO as ultimate responsible person, 3% total-revenue surcharge, CISO independence; stalled at subcommittee since March 2026), Rep. Lee Hae-min's Network Act bill (burden-of-proof reversal, punitive damages), two PPP national cybersecurity basic act bills (Kim Sang-hoon, late 2025; Eom Tae-young, 2026-09-28), the PIPA CEO-accountability bill, and the Information Security Industry Act decree expanding disclosure to all listed companies (planned 2027-01-01). The October 2026 bank attacks (seven institutions, Shinhan 25,729 customers) have so far produced emergency orders, a 28-officer police team, a 2026-10-19 audit of five bank CEOs and a proposed class action, not new statute. No 2026 CIIP Act amendment was found.

### Cited Findings

Breach notification timelines (in force)
- PIPA Enforcement Decree Art. 40 (amended 2026-05-19): report to the PIPC or KISA within 72 hours of becoming aware of a leak of 1,000 or more people, of sensitive or unique identifiers, or any leak from unlawful external access; notify data subjects within 72 hours; fine for non-report up to KRW 30 million under PIPA Art. 75(2)(18) — [개인정보보호위원회, 개인정보 유출신고제도 (PIPC, "Personal data breach reporting system")](https://pipc.go.kr/np/default/page.do?mCode=D030040000); [법률사무소 인평, 개인정보 유출, 과징금과 배상책임 (Inpyeong law office, "Data leaks: surcharges and liability")](https://inpyeonglaw.com/archives/28038/)
- Network Act: breach incidents (hacking, malware) must be reported to MSIT or KISA within 24 hours of awareness; the two reports are separate filings — [법률사무소 인평 (Inpyeong)](https://inpyeonglaw.com/archives/28038/)
- Reporting speed is now a direct factor in PIPA surcharge computation; prompt notification and containment earn the maximum mitigation credit — [머니투데이, 개인정보 유출 72시간…신고 지연은 과징금 전체를 흔드는 변수 (Money Today, "72 hours: late reporting shakes the whole surcharge")](https://www.mt.co.kr/industry/2026/09/29/2026092913220431784)

Electronic Financial Transactions Act and FSC/FSS rules; the 5-5-7 rule
- The 5-5-7 rule: at least 5% of all staff in IT, at least 5% of IT staff in security, at least 7% of the IT budget on security; introduced in 2011 as a recommended floor in the Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulation (전자금융감독규정) — [ZDNet Korea, 금융보안 '5·5·7' 기준, 가이드서도 유지 ("5-5-7 standard retained in the guide")](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20200102100835)
- The rule sunset on 2020-01-01 and moved into the Financial Security Institute's voluntary Financial Security Governance Guide, which kept the same numbers — [바이라인네트워크, 금융사 IT·보안 '5･5･7' 기준 효력 일몰, 완전 자율로 (Byline Network, "5-5-7 sunsets; fully voluntary")](https://byline.network/2020/01/2-54/); [BIkorea, 효력 상실한 '5·5·7', 금융권 향후 대응은 ("5-5-7 lapses; what next for finance")](https://m.bikorea.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=25592)
- On 2025-02-05 the FSC adopted a rules-to-principles rewrite of the Supervisory Regulation (293 prescriptive items cut to 166); Art. 8(2) now requires "personnel with expertise in IT and information security and sufficient budget" in place of the 5%/5%/7% figures; the information security committee's board-reporting duty (Art. 8-2(4)) applies from 2025-08-05 — [금융위원회 보도자료, 「전자금융감독규정」 일부개정고시안 금융위원회 의결 (FSC press release, "FSC resolves partial amendment of the Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulation")](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/83954?srchCtgry=&curPage=&srchKey=&srchText=&srchBeginDt=&srchEndDt=); [Kim & Chang, 개정 '전자금융감독규정' 시행 ("Amended Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulation in force")](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=31385); [광장 뉴스레터, 「전자금융감독규정」 개정 (Lee & Ko newsletter, "Amendment of the Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulation")](https://www.leeko.com/newsl/digitalfinance/202502_2/202502.pdf); [위키문헌, 전자금융감독규정 (제2025-4호) (Wikisource, Regulation No. 2025-4)](https://ko.wikisource.org/wiki/%EC%A0%84%EC%9E%90%EA%B8%88%EC%9C%B5%EA%B0%90%EB%8F%85%EA%B7%9C%EC%A0%95_(%EC%A0%9C2025-4%ED%98%B8))
- Conflict: an October 2026 Dailian article states the Supervisory Regulation "merely" sets security budget at 7% of IT budget and staff at 5% of IT staff, which contradicts the 2025 principle-based text; this appears to describe the pre-2025 wording — [데일리안, AI 해킹에 실시간 뚫리는 금융권…정부 대응은 '사후약방문' (Dailian, "Finance breached in real time by AI hacking; government response after the fact")](https://www.dailian.co.kr/news/view/1698337/)
- The FSC's 2025 governance guide revision set "IT and security scale" criteria — [대한금융신문, 금융보안 거버넌스 가이드 개정…'IT·보안 규모' 기준 제시 (Korea Financial Times, "Governance guide revised; IT and security scale criteria")](https://www.kbanker.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=88576)
- After the October 2026 hacks the 5-5-7 rule is back in debate as the minimum investment benchmark; major banks reportedly met it yet were breached, and banks spent about KRW 270 billion a year without stopping AI-driven attacks; no revival into binding rules was found — [인베스트조선, 예산 늘리면 뭐하나…연 2700억 쓰고도 AI 해킹 못 막은 은행들 (Invest Chosun, "What good is a bigger budget? - Financial regulators' July 2025 plan (FSC/FSS press release 2025-07-30): self-checks on ransomware readiness, themed backup inspections and blind penetration tests, punitive surcharges for serious incidents caused by inadequate security, stronger CISO authority, and an integrated threat monitoring system — [금융위원회 보도자료, 금융권 침해사고가 재발하지 않도록 철저히 대비 (FSC press release, "Thoroughly preparing so financial-sector breaches do not recur")](https://fsc.go.kr/no010101/85029?curPage=2&srchBeginDt=8&srchCtgry=&srchEndDt=&srchKey=&srchText=)

Critical information infrastructure (정보통신기반보호법)
- Under the CIIP Act Art. 8, heads of central administrative agencies designate critical information and communications infrastructure based on dependence, interconnection and the scale of harm from an incident; under Art. 8-2 MSIT and the NIS may recommend designations and (Decree Art. 13) form designation survey teams — [국가법령정보센터, 정보통신기반 보호법 제6조 등 (law.go.kr, CIIP Act)](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=900625834&lsId=009182&chrClsCd=010202&print=print); [KISA, 민간분야 주요정보통신기반시설 보호 (KISA, "Protection of private-sector critical infrastructure")](https://www.kisa.or.kr/1051204)

## Key question 5: Where does Korea stand against the US, EU and Japan on executive accountability for security?

### Takeaway
Korea now sits between the EU and the US: like NIS2 it imposes governance duties (executive CISO, board reporting, committee) and revenue-based fines, but unlike NIS2 Article 20 it does not yet make the management body personally liable or expose executives to management bans; its explicit CEO-responsibility provisions remain in pending PIPA and EFTA bills. The US relies on four-business-day SEC incident disclosure with no governance mandate, and the SEC's attempt to hold a CISO personally liable (SolarWinds) collapsed. Japan's 2025 Active Cyber Defense law is a state-capability and critical-infrastructure reporting statute with no executive-liability component.

### Inferences
- Measured by enacted law, Korea's executive accountability is "institutional" (a named executive CISO with board access and a committee, plus corporate fines up to 10% of revenue) rather than "personal" (NIS2-style liability of directors or bans); if the Yoo Dong-soo EFTA bill and the PIPA CEO-accountability bill pass, Korea would move closer to NIS2 than any other Asian jurisdiction.
- Korea's breach-notification windows (24 hours to MSIT/KISA; 72 hours to PIPC) are tighter than the SEC's four business days and comparable to NIS2's 24-hour early warning and 72-hour notification.
