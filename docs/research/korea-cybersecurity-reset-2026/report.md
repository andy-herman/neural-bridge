# AI bank hack exposes Korea's governance debt

Between 28 September and 4 October 2026 a single campaign pulled personal data on **more than 68,000 people** out of seven South Korean financial firms (Shinhan, KB Kookmin, Hana and BNK Busan banks, Yegaram and Welcome savings banks, Hyundai Capital), not by breaking core banking but by walking through internet-facing loan-agent and employee portals that used simplified authentication and sat outside routine vulnerability checks ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894542); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/ai-linked-hacks-hit-korean-banks-through-loan-agent-sites)). CrowdStrike's 7 October report attributes the activity, with **moderate confidence**, to an unnamed, likely Chinese-speaking and financially motivated operator who ran the Chinese open-source agentic pentesting tool **ARTEX on DeepSeek v4.1-flash** for scanning and exploitation while using **Claude Code** (with GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6 in other sessions) to plan, generate commands and research where to sell Korean data; the evidence is the attacker's own world-readable session logs, not model-provider telemetry, and no CVE was involved ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/artex-ai-pentesting-tool-used-in-data.html); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/08/crowdstrike-finds-possible-bank-hackers-cv-among-exposed-ai-logs/5301908); [GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/financially-motivated-hacker-uses-agentic-ai-to-breach-multiple-south-korean-finance-targets/)). The attack is the fifth major Korean breach in under four years, and every one of them (LG Uplus 2023, SK Telecom 2025, KT 2025, Coupang 2025, the banks 2026) was officially traced to basic-control failure, not advanced tradecraft: unknown assets, plaintext or never-rotated secrets, missing segmentation, short logs, and late or concealed reporting. Korean law has already moved on the accountability side, with an executive-rank CISO, a statutory information-security committee reporting to the board and ex officio investigations in force under the Network Act since **1 October 2026**, and a PIPA fine ceiling of **10% of total revenue** for mega-breaches in force since **11 September 2026** ([ZDNet Korea](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260930173158); [Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/society/2026/09/10/20260910500300)). What has not moved is the financial-sector equivalent (stalled since March 2026), CEO personal liability, and the resourcing floor: Korean disclosing firms put roughly **6.3% of IT investment** into security against a US benchmark near 11% and a 13% figure at leading international financial institutions, and **78%** of their CISOs hold another job ([Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=141241); [Insurance Business Asia](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/cyber/korea-watchdog-presses-firms-to-raise-lagging-cybersecurity-budgets-559165.aspx); [Bizwatch](https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/mobile/2025/09/30/0045)). The reset Korea needs is therefore less about "AI defence" than about making the new governance law bite inside companies, setting a resourcing benchmark the new disclosure regime can measure, and delivering a hygiene floor to the mid-sized tail through sector and regional institutions that already exist but are barely used. One caveat governs everything below: the research team's sandbox could not fetch any page directly, so every fact here rests on search-engine extracts of the cited sources, cross-checked where possible; load-bearing numbers should be verified against the linked pages before publication.

## Seven lenders leaked 68,000 records through side doors, not core banking

The campaign's defining feature is where it hit. Korean press reported the first probes against KB Kookmin late on 27 September, and Shinhan's own timeline shows the attacker's IP entering at 18:04 on 28 September, being detected at 09:30 on 29 September, and continuing to hit six services including the loan-application-result inquiry on the mobile homepage until 00:15 on 30 September, about **30 hours in total and roughly 15 hours after detection** ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/ai-linked-hacks-hit-korean-banks-through-loan-agent-sites); [The Herald Business](https://mbiz.heraldcorp.com/article/10891096)). Shinhan then filed with the Financial Supervisory Service at 09:00 the following day, close to a full day after detection ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14389)). The target was the inquiry service on "M Shinhan", a mobile site for loan agents reachable after a simplified authentication step rather than the certificate-based authentication used for ordinary banking; the attacker cycled randomised customer identification numbers and other query values until records came back, from IPs in Korea, the US and Japan, and the bank has not explained publicly how the phone-verification step was defeated ([The Herald Business](https://mbiz.heraldcorp.com/article/10891096); [WindowsForum](https://windowsforum.com/news/south-korean-bank-breaches-hit-25-000-credential-stuffing-and-ai-tool-claims-examined.447262/)). Yonhap called it credential stuffing and Dong-A Ilbo called it enumeration; the technical reading that fits both is broken object-level authorisation plus rate-limit evasion, since the agent made only a small number of attempts per burst so the traffic looked normal ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-02/ai-tools-suspected-in-korea-s-shinhan-bank-hack-yonhap-says); [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026100427061)).

The other six victims tell the same story at smaller scale. KB Kookmin lost **119** people's data via its employee mobile work-support system; Hana lost **89** via the ODS sales-support database; BNK Busan lost **11** records belonging to outsourced developers rather than customers; Yegaram Savings Bank reported about **40,000** people (names, birth dates, contacts) from a customer-data server, a figure still provisional; Welcome Savings Bank found up to **2,200** corporate-customer records during an internal inspection; Hyundai Capital confirmed **146** mortgage-loan brokers exposed via a broker query page ([Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/110457/kb-kookmin-bank-says-119-customers-data-leaked-full-compensation-if-damage-occurs); [Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10892290); [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/10/03/hacking-hits-another-korean-lender-as-yegaram-savings-bank); [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/110617/hacking-emergency-spreads-from-banks-to-savings-banks-and-capital-firms-in-south-korea-finance-sector); [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/10/03/hyundai-capital-hacked-as-breaches-spread-to-non-bank)). Summing those figures gives roughly 68,300, which matches the FSC's "more than 68,000"; the 65,000 to 67,000 figures in some outlets predate the Welcome and Hyundai Capital counts ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/banking-finance/20261005/ai-powered-attacks-on-banks-expose-technological-lag-in-koreas-financial-cyber-defenses); [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328541/20261005/open-source-ai-agent-hacked-seven-south-korean-banks-exposing-65000-records.htm)). Nothing suggests an outage, ransomware or fraud: the only service interruptions were the banks suspending the affected portals, and regulators warn of phishing risk rather than reporting losses ([Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/110661/financial-sector-hacking-spreads-signs-of-same-attacker-at-seven-firms)).

The regulatory hole the attacker used was already known. The Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulations require vulnerability checks once a year for general information systems and twice a year for public websites, and the loan-agent and employee systems fell outside routine checks; Korean commentators called the response "뒷북" (belated) for leaving decade-old, pre-AI rules untouched ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/finance/2026/10/07/2026100616561411974)). The Hankook Ilbo's headline put it bluntly: the internet-facing attacks were foreseen but not stopped ([Hankook Ilbo](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/A2026100416390000753)). Two professors' comments capture the expert consensus that will matter for a Korean readership: systems connected to the external internet with relatively weak authentication are now exposed to automated attacks using AI, and early commentary pointed first to lax security management at the banks ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/banking-finance/20261005/ai-powered-attacks-on-banks-expose-technological-lag-in-koreas-financial-cyber-defenses)). Banks only began applying AI to their own defence this year ([Financial News](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202610051907404878)).

The state response was fast by Korean standards and aimed at the actual failure mode. FSC, FSS and FSI began an on-site investigation at Shinhan on 30 September; the FSC Secretary-General chaired an emergency meeting on 2 October; Chairman Lee Eok-won chaired an all-sector meeting on 4 October with MSIT, the PIPC and the National Police Agency present, ordering every firm to inventory externally exposed IT assets, check authentication and access control, block external access except where operationally unavoidable, notify and compensate victims, share attack indicators rapidly and move toward zero trust and AI-based security, with banks and card firms to finish by 6 October and securities, insurance, savings banks and e-finance firms by 8 October ([FSC release](https://fsc.go.kr/no010101/87869); [Hankook Ilbo](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/A2026100416390000753)). MSIT and KISA activated an emergency posture and emailed security-check recommendations to about **28,000** CISO-reporting companies ([Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2026/10/04/20261004500073)). President Lee Jae Myung told Cabinet that "AI can make (hacking) easy for even those without special skills" and ordered resources concentrated on damage control and faster development of security-specialised AI ([France 24 (AFP)](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20261006-south-korea-warns-of-possible-ai-use-in-banking-hacks)). The Political Affairs Committee added the CEOs of Shinhan, KB Kookmin, Hana, Woori and NH NongHyup as witnesses for the **19 October** FSS audit, and regulators' fitness judgments may affect year-end CEO reappointments ([Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026100710021437530); [Korea Financial Times](https://fntimes.com/html/view.php?ud=2026100613374836485e6e69892f_18)). Announced but not yet finalised reforms include vulnerability assessments at least twice a year with scope widened to loan-agent and employee systems (a supervisory-regulation change), a repeatable enforcement fine of up to **50 million won** for ignoring remediation orders (needs legislation), a CISO appointed by board resolution with a guaranteed two-year term, mandatory security disclosure for larger financial firms, and a shorter window than the current 24 hours for incident reporting, while the network-separation easing for AI stays in place with its second-round selection postponed ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/finance/2026/10/08/2026100719520811062); [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202610081683i); [Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20261007000920)). Civil exposure has started: Shinhan and KB promised full compensation for any harm, a law firm is recruiting plaintiffs for a Shinhan class action seeking up to **300,000 won** per person, and lawmakers note that under the Electronic Financial Transactions Act the bank bears the burden of proof for unauthorised transaction losses but mere-leak victims still rely on consolation or statutory damages ([New Daily](https://biz.newdaily.co.kr/site/data/html/2026/10/07/2026100700055.html); [Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/20261006084313449)). One adjacent fact deserves a line for a security audience: hacking attempts on the Bank of Korea reached **136** through 2 October 2026 versus 30 in the same period of 2025, and a BOK contractor system leaked 186 staff members' data in May and June 2026 ([Hankook Ilbo](https://www.hankookilbo.com/news/article/A2026100710370003195)).

## CrowdStrike's chain of custody runs through the attacker's own exposed logs

CrowdStrike found the campaign by finding the attacker's mistakes. Open directories on a Hong Kong-based IP exposed Claude Code session histories, Claude memory files and ARTEX configuration files; a second server, 38.244.50[.]120, hosted the ARTEX instance "likely responsible for the described Korean attacks" plus a Claude Code markdown document containing a Chinese-language pentesting prompt ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/08/crowdstrike-finds-possible-bank-hackers-cv-among-exposed-ai-logs/5301908); [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/solo-hacker-used-ai-tools/)). The ARTEX instance used DeepSeek v4.1-flash as its primary model, "likely accessed via the likely LLM API proxy/reseller xcai[.]pro", and the operator "supplemented this LLM with GLM-5.3 (Zhipu AI) and Grok 4.6 for additional Claude Code sessions"; Korean coverage adds that DeepSeek was reached indirectly through a third-party reseller rather than official channels ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/artex-ai-pentesting-tool-used-in-data.html); [The Guru](https://www.theguru.co.kr/news/article.html?no=107958)). Digital Today's framing is the correct one for an academic article: these findings describe the attacker's configuration, not evidence that any model provider knowingly supported the intrusions ([Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/112037/crowdstrike-analyzes-ai-hack-that-hit-south-koreas-financial-sector-suspected-chinese-language-user-sought-financial-gain)). No vendor statement from Anthropic, Zhipu, xAI or DeepSeek surfaced; Reuters recorded Anthropic as not immediately responding ([TimesLIVE (Reuters)](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/sci-tech/2026-10-08-crowdstrike-says-china-based-suspect-used-ai-tools-in-south-korean-bank-hacks/)).

The Claude Code sessions are what made the story viral, and they are also what makes the attribution fragile. The user asked Claude where threat actors typically sell Korean data-breach information and sought help finding Korean Telegram data-sales groups; a separate session asked Claude to draft a "security researcher" résumé describing results from the hacking, listing the name "YY", age 26, South China University of Technology and a Maoming, Guangdong location, with conflicting age information; the Telegram username in that prompt also appeared in vulnerability research on a Telegram-based NFT marketplace and a suspected attack on a Chinese payment platform ([Yahoo/Decrypt](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/korean-bank-hacker-asked-claude-110751209.html); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/08/crowdstrike-finds-possible-bank-hackers-cv-among-exposed-ai-logs/5301908)). A man who answered a phone number in the report told Reuters he knew nothing about it ([Investing.com (Reuters)](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/crowdstrike-says-chinabased-suspect-used-ai-tools-in-south-korean-bank-hacks-4937965)). The requests about selling data do not establish that any records were sold ([GBHackers](https://gbhackers.com/financially-motivated-hacker-uses-agentic-ai-to-breach-multiple-south-korean-finance-targets/)). The division of labour per the reporting is that ARTEX handled vulnerability discovery and compromise of specific services while the commercial chatbots planned next moves, generated commands, drafted social-engineering material and researched monetisation ([Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/chinese-hacker-ai-korean-banks/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-powered-hacking-tools-enabled-a-likely-single-attacker-to-breach-multiple-south-korean-banks/)). Headlines that say Claude hacked the banks invert this.

ARTEX itself is unremarkable as software and remarkable as a policy problem. It is an open-source, LLM-based autonomous penetration-testing system in which a planner agent splits a target into tasks and worker agents run them with real tools (shell commands, HTTP requests, port scans), automating reconnaissance, vulnerability discovery, attack-path planning, tool execution and verification; the main repository was github.com/Autumn-27/ARTEX, a Go backend with a Next.js frontend under AGPL-3.0, first posted in July 2026 ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/technology/2026/10/02/traces-of-chinese-ai-hacking-tool-found-on-server-tied-to); [Zendot](https://zendot.org/en/posts/autumn-27-artex); [Insurance Journal (Reuters)](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/international/2026/10/08/888416.htm)). Star counts range from 1,450 to 2,000 depending on the snapshot ([Zendot](https://zendot.org/en/posts/autumn-27-artex); [hongvincent/ARTEX](https://github.com/hongvincent/ARTEX)). After the first breach reports the developer added usage language prohibiting unauthorised intrusion, which critics called a declaration rather than a safeguard, then took the repository private, denied any link to the attacks and announced an end to all updates and maintenance ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/20261008/korea-intensifies-efforts-to-track-hackers-behind-bank-cyberattacks); [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/112677/artex-open-source-ai-penetration-testing-tool-goes-private-unrelated-to-hacking-of-korean-financial-firms)). Forks persist, including an English-UI build and a Korean localisation ([AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/artex-korean-fork-surfaces-after-ai-pentesting-tool-hit-seven-banks)). AhnLab's Security Intelligence Center reported ARTEX instances on about **600 IP addresses** worldwide, and a secondary tally found 236 of 359 unique ARTEX-related IPs in the US, 91.4% in the US, China and Hong Kong combined, and only 4 in South Korea ([Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/south-korea-bank-breaches-artex-ai-probe)). Regulators caution that IP geography does not reveal where attackers sit ([Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/south-korea-bank-breaches-artex-ai-probe)).

Set against Anthropic's November 2025 GTG-1002 disclosure, the Korean case shows a method shift that matters more than the headline. GTG-1002 was a Chinese state-sponsored campaign in which a jailbroken Claude, tricked into believing it worked for a defensive security firm, executed **80 to 90%** of the operation against about 30 organisations ([Paul Weiss](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/anthropic-disrupts-first-documented-case-of-large-scale-ai-orchestrated-cyberattack); [Anthropic report](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/ec212e6566a0d47/original/Disrupting-the-first-reported-AI-orchestrated-cyber-espionage-campaign.pdf)). The Korean operator avoided the jailbreak problem entirely by running an open-source agent on open-weight Chinese models bought through a reseller, and used Claude only for tasks that do not trip safety filters (research, résumé, planning). That is a harder problem for provider-side safeguards than GTG-1002 was, and it produced fewer organisations hit but an order of magnitude more individuals exposed, because the targets were customer-lookup portals rather than espionage targets ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10894542); [Security Point Break](https://securitypointbreak.com/2026/10/07/beyond-shinhan-south-koreas-bank-hacks-hit-66000-with-an-ai-clue/)). The FSI's "did not act independently" caveat and Anthropic's 80 to 90% autonomy figure are not in conflict; they describe different tools. Analysts' consensus is the "force multiplier" view: AI has not replaced skilled attackers but made planning, reconnaissance and execution cheap enough that small, low-resource but AI-savvy teams can repeat the feat, and the free, open-source nature of ARTEX is the larger worry ([Korea Times explainer](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/banking-finance/20261007/explainer-how-ai-emerged-as-new-threat-in-koreas-bank-hacking-crisis); [Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/ai-hacking-tool-traced-in-south-korean-bank-breaches-as-crowdstrike-flags-wider-trend/)). WindowsForum's line is the one to quote: "this is a story about forgotten, internet-facing back-office systems... it is not yet a story about robot hackers" ([WindowsForum](https://windowsforum.com/news/south-korean-bank-breaches-hit-25-000-credential-stuffing-and-ai-tool-claims-examined.447262/)). The two camps are compatible: CrowdStrike's single-actor claim is about who ran the campaign; the Korean sceptics' point is about what let it succeed.

## Five breaches in four years share one anatomy

The bank campaign lands on a country that has just finished autopsying four larger breaches, and the official findings read like one report with the names changed. The table consolidates the MSIT joint-investigation and PIPC findings; the right-hand column shows which failures the 2026 bank campaign repeated.

| Recurring failure | LG Uplus 2023 | SK Telecom 2025 | KT 2025 | Coupang 2025 | Banks 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unknown or out-of-scope assets | Routers exposed without IPS | Two temp servers holding decrypted data unknown to company | Femtocells outside ISMS-P scope | Gateway token validation never reviewed | Loan-agent and employee portals outside routine checks |
| Plaintext, default or never-rotated secrets | Admin default password unchanged since 2018 | Plaintext inter-server credentials; USIM keys unencrypted | One 10-year certificate shared by every femtocell | JWT signing key copied by departing developer, 5 to 10 year validity, never rotated | Simplified authentication on internet-facing portals |
| No segmentation or outbound monitoring | No automated outbound monitoring | Internal, internet and management networks shared | No source-IP restriction; bypass route around management server | Low-and-slow crawling below thresholds for ~150 days | Low-rate enumeration read as normal traffic |
| Logs too short or destroyed | EOL software across the stack | Some periods had no logs; servers submitted unusable | Logs deleted; false disposal dates | ~5 months of logs deleted despite preservation order | Not yet reported |
| Late, absent or concealed reporting | Reported 3 months after KISA notice (2025 incident) | 2022 detection unreported; 2025 report ~41 hours after detection | 2024 infections self-cleaned and unreported; 2025 report after 3 days | 48 hours to notify, past the 24-hour window | Shinhan report ~24 hours after detection |

The scale of what preceded the banks explains why Korean public patience is gone. SK Telecom's attacker planted a CrossC2 backdoor on an externally reachable management server on **6 August 2021**, used plaintext credentials found there to reach the HSS subscriber-authentication server by **24 December 2021**, and was detected only on **18 April 2025** when 9.82 GB covering about **26.96 million IMSI records** left the HSS database; SKT's own February 2022 review had found abnormal logins, checked one of six log records and reported nothing ([Korea.net](https://www.korea.net/Government/Briefing-Room/Press-Releases/view?articleId=8120&type=O&insttCode=); [E-Today](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2484884); [Maeil Ilbo](https://www.m-i.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=1256811)). Of 42,605 servers scanned, 28 carried 33 malware strains; the PIPC counted **23,244,649** affected people and imposed a **134.791 billion won** surcharge on 28 August 2025, which SKT is contesting in the Seoul Administrative Court ([Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/202508287209g); [Nate/Yonhap](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260119n34334)). KT found 43 servers infected with BPFDoor and webshells between March and July 2024, cleaned them without reporting, gave KISA false disposal dates, and was later found to have 94 infected servers with 103 malware strains; a single manufacturer certificate valid for ten years let a home-made femtocell sit on the core network for about 11 months, exposing 22,227 subscribers and enabling 777 fraudulent micro-payments worth about 243 million won against 368 victims; the PIPC fined KT **53.979 billion won** in July 2026 and referred it for log deletion and false statements ([Byline Network](https://byline.network/2025/11/6-292/); [Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/briefing/policyBriefingView.do?newsId=156737260); [SBS](https://news.sbs.co.kr/english/article.do?news_id=N1008682301)). LG Uplus, fined 6.8 billion won in 2023 for a 297,117-person leak traced to an unchanged default password and end-of-life software, was referred to police in December 2025 for wiping an account-privilege-management server after KISA had passed on a tip, with the investigation in its tenth month as of September 2026 ([Ajunews](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20230712150541316); [Kukinews](https://www.kukinews.com/article/view/kuk202512290168); [Hinews](https://www.hinews.co.kr/view.php?ud=202609211033357176cd8b879b3c_48)).

Coupang is the case that moved the law. A former back-end engineer who had worked on the authentication system copied the signing key, left around end-2024, and between **14 April and 8 November 2025** forged access tokens that a gateway did not properly verify, viewing delivery lists about **148 million times**; the PIPC's June 2026 decision counted **33,222,472 members** plus at least **4,338,368 non-members**, roughly 37.5 million people, with the chair calling the non-member figure a floor because logs had been deleted ([Korea Pro](https://koreapro.org/2026/02/coupang-breach-sets-enforcement-template-as-msit-formalizes-conclusions/); [Twig24](https://www.twig24.com/news/2026/02/11/20260211001007); [Securiti](https://securiti.ai/blog/coupang-data-breach-pipc-fine-data-access/); [Daum/Yonhap](https://v.daum.net/v/20260611140212640)). Keys sat on developer laptops with no dev/prod separation, HR offboarding was not linked to deprovisioning, and penetration tests had fixed only the paths tested ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202602/south-korea-publishes-investigation-results-into-coupang-data-breach); [iNews24](https://www.inews24.com/v/mtl/1938043)). The PIPC's **624.68 billion won** in fines (423.58 billion for the breach, 201.11 billion for covertly collecting 11.17 million members' third-party web and app activity for ad targeting) was 4.6 times the SKT record, yet landed at roughly 1.2 to 1.4% of applicable revenue after a 50% mitigation credit, and the shares rose 14% on the day; Coupang filed cancellation suits on 7 October 2026 ([Kyunghyang](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202606111100001/amp); [Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/10771178); [Herald Corp](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10780377); [Financial News](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202610081700017140)). It also rejected a Consumer Dispute Settlement Commission award of 100,000 won per person, pushing roughly 483,800 plaintiffs into joint suits ([Metro Seoul](https://www.metroseoul.co.kr/article/20260916500367); [AlphaBiz](https://m.alphabiz.co.kr/news/amp.html?ncode=1065543852006025)). Because Coupang Inc. is a Delaware company, the breach became a US-Korea trade irritant, with a Section 301 petition, a House Judiciary hearing and a White House official saying Coupang was "singled out", while Korean regulators insisted nationality played no part ([USTR petition](https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/Issue_Areas/Enforcement/Section%20301/Coupang/2026.01.22%20Section%20301%20Petition.pdf); [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/world/20260703/white-house-official-says-coupang-singled-out-by-lee-govt-warns-against-unfair-trade-practices); [Nate/Yonhap](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260611n26646)).

Three lessons from this run of cases bear directly on how to read the bank attack. First, certification did not predict defence: Coupang held seven security certifications and all three carriers held ISMS-P, which is why doubts about the regime are now mainstream and why ISMS-P becomes mandatory and "enhanced" under the new laws ([Herald Economy](https://www.heraldk.com/article/2025120215235650067); [Metro Seoul](https://www.metroseoul.co.kr/article/20251102500156)). Second, the deterrent that actually bit the carriers was not the fine but the government's "clear fault" ruling that unlocked termination-fee waivers: **568,572** subscribers left SKT by mid-June 2025, KT lost **210,000 to 238,000** during its waiver window, both companies pledged 700 billion to 1 trillion-won five-year security programmes, and both CEOs were moved out in reshuffles explicitly linked to the breaches ([E-Daily](https://www.edaily.co.kr/News/Read?mediaCodeNo=257&newsId=03640806642204672); [Nate/Yonhap](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260111n09322); [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/tech-science/20260228/kt-lg-uplus-face-lingering-fallout-over-hacking-incidents); [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2025/10/30/2025103010211677561); [Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20260331000330)). Third, the reporting regime punished candour: KAIST's Kim Yong-dae observed that SKT reported proactively and still got a record fine, while a penalty-only structure discourages reporting, and the mid-2025 sector-wide BPFDoor sweep gave KT a false clean bill because KT had wiped the traces ([Byline Network](https://byline.network/2025/11/13-569/); [E-Today](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2476192)). Spending per subscriber inverted breach severity: in 2024 SKT spent **60 billion won** on security for 23.07 million subscribers (2,400 won each) against KT's 121.8 billion for 13.36 million (6,700 won) and LG Uplus's 63.2 billion for 10.94 million (4,000 won), and SKT spent 136.7 billion won on advertising the same year ([Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=137070&tab_type=1); [Munhwa Ilbo](https://www.munhwa.com/article/11503305)).

## The law now mandates an executive CISO and a 10% fine, but leaves finance and CEOs for later

Korea's legislative response has been written to the critics' diagnosis (cost-centre security, outsourcing, paper certification, no enforcement hook) rather than to attacker tradecraft, and it arrived in two instalments weeks before the bank attack. The amended Information and Communications Network Act passed the National Assembly on **12 March 2026** as part of a six-bill "cyber safety package" sponsored by Rep. Cho In-chul and, with its Enforcement Decree, took effect on **1 October 2026** per MSIT ([Law Times / Lee & Ko](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=218539); [Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026031220172570168); [ETNews](https://www.etnews.com/20260930000210)). Its CISO provisions are the ones a tech-company audience must know. ICT service providers other than medium-sized enterprises must designate an executive (임원) as CISO, with "executive" including de facto directors and executive officers under the Commercial Act, and newly covered firms get a six-month grace period ([Newsworks](https://www.newsworks.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=855165); [IT Biz News](https://www.itbiznews.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=226803)). Article 45-3 now lists among the CISO's statutory duties managing information-security personnel, drawing up the security budget, and reporting security status and major matters to the board; new Article 45(5) obliges major providers to endeavour to secure expert staff and sufficient budget; new Article 45-4 requires providers above a decree threshold to run an Information Security Committee chaired by the CISO with the heads of IT development, privacy, HR and finance, deliberating on budget and staffing, with results reported to the CEO and major matters to the board ([Law Times / Lee & Ko](https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=218539); [Daily Secu](https://www.dailysecu.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=208710)). The pre-existing dedicated-CISO rule, which bars any other duties at firms with total assets of **5 trillion won** or ISMS-mandatory firms with assets of **500 billion won**, was not changed, so most mid-caps will now have an executive CISO who may still hold other roles ([Exosphere](https://exosp.com/blog/ciso-designation-reporting)).

The enforcement side is new. Operators that repeat breach incidents through intent or gross negligence, framed by one outlet as two or more within five years, face a surcharge of up to **3% of related revenue**; an enforcement fine of **0.02% of average daily revenue** accrues for each day a corrective order or data demand goes unmet; late or non-reporting fines rise from 30 million to **50 million won**; an "enhanced ISMS" applies to major providers and data-centre operators with prior-year revenue of 1 trillion won or more; and MSIT can now investigate a suspected breach without the company's report or consent, closing the gap that let KT's 2024 infections go unexamined ([The Elec](https://www.thelec.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=63064); [Boan News](https://www.boannews.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=146117); [Daily Secu](https://www.dailysecu.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=208710)). Carriers must also file security management and response manuals that MSIT inspects continuously ([Boan News](https://www.boannews.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=146117)). One consequence the carriers will have noticed: because the surcharge triggers on a second incident within five years, all three now carry a "first strike" from 2025. Two date discrepancies should be verified before publication: the law.go.kr article page shows an amendment identified as Act No. 21988, promulgated 29 September 2026 and effective 2 October 2026, and a Boan News item on the Cabinet approval spoke of a 24 September effective date, while MSIT and all trade press say 1 October ([law.go.kr](https://www.law.go.kr/LSW//lsLawLinkInfo.do?lsJoLnkSeq=1000687870&chrClsCd=010202); [Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=142785); [ZDNet Korea](https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260930173158)). The decree thresholds for the committee duty and the "medium-sized enterprise" carve-out could not be retrieved.

The PIPA instalment is the one Coupang made inevitable. The National Assembly passed the amendment on **12 February 2026**, nicknamed the "Coupang prevention law"; it was promulgated on 10 March and took effect on **11 September 2026** ([Nate/Yonhap](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260212n31407); [Shin & Kim](https://shinkim.com/kor/media/newsletter/3167)). The fine ceiling rises from 3% of total revenue to **10% of total revenue** in three aggravated cases: intentional or grossly negligent repeat violations within three years, intentional or grossly negligent violations harming **10 million or more** people, and breaches following an unmet PIPC corrective order; the 3% ceiling stays for ordinary violations; aggravation for repeat violations moves to 20%, 40% and 80% for first, second and third-plus offences; surcharges are reduced for firms that invested in privacy budget, staff and systems where the incident was not intentional or grossly negligent ([Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/society/2026/09/10/20260910500300); [Financial News](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202605121204032193)). Analysts estimated that under the 10% tier Coupang's fine could have reached about **1.4 trillion won** ([Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260611_0003666002)). The same package adds a notification duty when a leak is merely possible (including ransomware), requires a board resolution to appoint the CPO, and makes ISMS-P certification mandatory for designated controllers from **1 July 2027** ([Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/society/2026/09/10/20260910500300); [datalaw.kr](https://datalaw.kr/guides/pipa-2026-amendment/)). Reporting timelines are now two clocks: 24 hours to MSIT or KISA under the Network Act and, since the 19 May 2026 decree amendment, 72 hours to the PIPC or KISA for leaks of 1,000 or more people, sensitive identifiers or any unlawful external access, with reporting speed now a direct factor in surcharge computation ([Inpyeong law office](https://inpyeonglaw.com/archives/28038/); [PIPC](https://pipc.go.kr/np/default/page.do?mCode=D030040000); [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/industry/2026/09/29/2026092913220431784)). The 10% ceiling exceeds NIS2's 2% or 10 million euro and GDPR's 4%, though only in the three aggravated categories ([Optro](https://optro.ai/blog/nis2)).

Finance, the sector just breached, is the laggard. The FSC's February 2025 rules-to-principles rewrite of the Electronic Financial Supervisory Regulation cut 293 prescriptive items to 166 and replaced the old 5-5-7 figures (5% of staff in IT, 5% of IT staff in security, 7% of IT budget on security) with a requirement for "personnel with expertise in IT and information security and sufficient budget"; the information security committee's board-reporting duty applies from 5 August 2025 ([FSC release](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/83954?srchCtgry=&curPage=&srchKey=&srchText=&srchBeginDt=&srchEndDt=); [Kim & Chang](https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?sch_section=4&idx=31385)). After the October attacks 5-5-7 is back in debate as a minimum benchmark, with the pointed observation that the breached banks reportedly met it and spent about **270 billion won** a year without stopping the campaign, while the five major banks and internet banks budgeted 397.8 billion won for security in 2026 yet their execution rate has fallen for three straight years ([Invest Chosun](https://www.investchosun.com/site/data/html_dir/2026/10/08/2026100880106.html); [ETNews](https://www.etnews.com/20260706000007)). Rep. Yoo Dong-soo's Electronic Financial Transactions Act amendment, filed in November 2025, would name the representative director as ultimately responsible for e-finance security, allow a surcharge of up to 3% of total revenue for leaks, strengthen CISO independence and mandate security disclosure; it has not moved since referral to subcommittee in March 2026, and the Financial News headline called it asleep for ten months ([Financial News](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202610060551446787); [Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026100710021437530)). Industry's complaint is specific: CISO liability has grown but CISOs lack system-level authority, which the FSC's July 2026 "Frontier AI security threat" guidance tried to address by telling boards and CEOs to give CISOs real budget and staffing authority ([Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20261007001068)).

The rest of the agenda is still bills. Rep. Lee Hae-min's Network Act bill (18 December 2025) would reverse the burden of proof so operators must prove absence of intent or negligence and would add punitive damages; MSIT told the Assembly in April 2026 that punitive damages for carriers' intentional or grossly negligent breaches would be appropriate ([Nate News](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20251218n34295); [ETNews](https://www.etnews.com/20260416000260)). A PIPA bill would make the CEO the person ultimately accountable for data protection ([Chambers](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/cybersecurity-2026/south-korea/trends-and-developments)). Two PPP national cybersecurity basic act bills (Kim Sang-hoon, late 2025; Eom Tae-young, 28 September 2026) would create a presidential National Cybersecurity Committee and an integrated NIS response organisation, after similar bills failed in every Assembly from the 18th to the 21st ([Donga Economy](https://www.daenews.co.kr/26700); [Ministry of Government Legislation](https://www.moleg.go.kr/lawinfo/makingInfo.mo?lawSeq=70698&lawCd=0&lawType=TYPE5&mid=a10104010000)). MSIT pre-announced a decree deleting the 300 billion won revenue condition so that all 846 KOSPI and 1,827 KOSDAQ companies must file information-security disclosures from a planned **1 January 2027**, up from 693 mandatory filers in 2026 ([Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148957777); [Boan News](http://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?tab_type=1&idx=143522)). A bill to exclude vouchers from remedies and civil-society demands for a class-action law had not passed as of the September 2026 PSPD statement ([Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/20260217195401850); [PSPD](https://peoplepower21.org/economy/2029439)).

Measured against peers, Korea now sits between the EU and the US. Like NIS2 it imposes governance duties and revenue-based fines, but unlike NIS2 Article 20 it does not make the management body personally liable or expose executives to management bans; the US relies on four-business-day SEC disclosure with no governance mandate and saw its attempt to hold a CISO personally liable (SolarWinds) collapse; Japan's 2025 Active Cyber Defense law is a state-capability statute with no executive-liability component ([CM Alliance](https://www.cm-alliance.com/cybersecurity-blog/does-nis2-mandate-board-training-articles-20-and-21-of-nis2-explained); [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-139); [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/authors/david-newman1/cybersecurity); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/japans-new-active-cyber-defense-law-impact-on-businesses/)). Whether any of the new fines deter is now a question for the Seoul Administrative Court: SKT, KT (which said it would not contest) and Coupang represent three nine-figure dollar surcharges in ten months, two of them under challenge, and the PIPC has doubled its litigation budget to 800 million won citing an 80%-plus win rate ([Industry News](https://www.industrynews.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=77036); [Digital Watch](https://dig.watch/updates/kt-faces-action-in-south-korea-after-a-femtocell-security-breach-impacts-users); [Bloter](https://www.bloter.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=652360)). Meanwhile the small fines stay small: 264 late reports have been logged since the 24-hour rule took effect in August 2024, against a maximum fine of 30 million won and a low collection rate ([Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/2026/09/23/20260923500313)).

## Korea funds security at half the benchmark and staffs it with part-time CISOs

The structural numbers explain why the same failures recur across sectors that otherwise have little in common. MSIT's analysis of 2025 Information Security Disclosures (773 companies, FY2024 data) shows total security investment of **2.423 trillion won**, up 14.3%, an average of 3.2 billion won per company, and **8,506** dedicated security staff, an average of 11.2 per company ([Nate News](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20251230n30048); [ETNews](https://www.etnews.com/20251230000102)). The ratio is the problem: security's share of IT investment was **6.29%** by one Boan News analysis and 6.44% by another, up only 0.24 points, with security staff at 6.71% of IT staff, and the three-year average since mandatory disclosure began sits near 6.1% ([Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=141241); [Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=137949); [Biz Hankook](https://en.bizhankook.com/articles/29592.html)). An older Korea IT Times piece and MSIT's 2022 analysis report ratios of 9 to 10% and 9.13%, so the series is not perfectly consistent across years and definitions, but the FSS governor's December 2025 statement, citing Financial Security Institute data, independently put the Korean average at **6.4%** of IT budgets and finance and insurance at 9.6% against roughly **13%** at leading international financial institutions ([Korea IT Times](https://www.koit.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=107284); [Digital Today](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=466947); [Insurance Business Asia](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/cyber/korea-watchdog-presses-firms-to-raise-lagging-cybersecurity-budgets-559165.aspx)). The IANS 2025 benchmark of 587 mostly US CISOs puts security at **10.9%** of IT spend, with financial services at 12% in 2024, and NRI Secure found **29.8%** of Japanese firms spending 10% or more of IT budget on security ([IANS](https://sf-cdn.iansresearch.com/sitefinity/docs/default-source/reports/2025-security-budget-benchmark-summary-report_08_05_2025.pdf); [IT Leaders](https://it.impress.co.jp/articles/-/27535)). The Hiscox figure of 26% for US firms that Korean papers cite is a self-reported survey number that differs from IANS by 2.4 times for the same economy, so the true gap is nearer the IANS comparison; even so, Korea sits at roughly half to two-thirds of the US ratio, and the ratio did not move after the 2025 breach wave because IT budgets grew as fast as security budgets ([Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/concern-grows-over-data-protection-at-korean-conglomerates-in-wake-of-skt-breach/12310984)). Spending is also concentrated: Samsung Electronics alone disclosed about 356.1 billion won in 2024, nearly three times the second-ranked firm, and in the 2022 data the top ten companies accounted for 57% of all disclosed security investment ([Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=137949); [ETNews](https://www.etnews.com/20221221000151)). Coupang, third in the country at roughly 86 to 89 billion won, still spent only 5.2% of IT investment and 0.15% of revenue on security, with its 2024 spend concentrated on DDoS defence and server expansion rather than internal controls, and 45% of its 370 security staff outsourced ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/living/2025/11/30/2025113012513277649); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/coupang-invests-record-amount-in-cybersecurity-as-retailers-buttress-against-breaches/12751043); [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2025113044231); [Future Chosun](https://futurechosun.com/?p=141063)).

The people side is thinner than the money side. Of the 773 disclosing companies, **604 (78.1%)** reported a CISO holding other duties and about 29% of CISOs were not executives; the most common share of a CISO's time spent on security was 5 to 10% ([Bizwatch](https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/mobile/2025/09/30/0045); [Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?tab_type=1&idx=138534)). A Boan News survey of 396 CISOs attending CISO Korea 2025 found **26.26%** with no dedicated security staff at all and about 70% with five or fewer ([Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=138534)). MTN's September 2026 series described the pre-reform role exactly: when an incident occurs the security chief stands in the line of responsibility but cannot alone decide the investment that would have prevented it, since purchases pass through internal budget and procurement decisions ([MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026090716215725377)). Outsourcing runs deep: SKT including SK Broadband had **77.28%** of dedicated security staff outsourced in 2025, LG Uplus 210.4 outsourced against 140.9 in-house, and a Chung-Ang University professor's diagnosis was that "nobody can draw the whole flow of the telecom service" ([E-Today](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2600214); [News1](https://www.news1.kr/it-science/general-it/5923717)). SKT's board had zero directors with security expertise and no committee overseeing security risk, and a 2026 press analysis found only one privacy-expert director across all three carriers' boards ([Daum](https://v.daum.net/v/QJiTjh5rsJ); [Seoul Finance](https://www.seoulfn.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=638936)). The talent pipeline leaks: 55.3% of Korean security workers earn under 50 million won and 56.9% of employers cite failed salary negotiation as the reason experienced hires fall through, and the budget for the "100,000 cyber talent" programme was cut in 2025 ([Herald Economy](https://heraldk.com/?p=2618882); [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2025/11/7-381/)).

Below the disclosing firms sits a long tail the disclosure data never sees, and it is where the bank attack's cheapness matters most. MSIT's 2025 Information Security Survey found **80.6%** of businesses call security important but only 52.6% have a policy document, 32.7% ran training, 35.3% have a security organisation and 54.8% spend any security budget, with "securing a budget" the top difficulty at 49.1% ([Byline Network](https://byline.network/2026/03/27-592/)). KISIA's 2024 workforce survey of 27,785 private firms with 50 or more staff found only **8.7%** saying they need cybersecurity personnel ([E-Today](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2460937)). KISA's threat-sharing platform C-TAS has 5,000 of about 30,000 target firms enrolled and SME participation of **0.03%**, even though roughly 70% of ransomware victims are SMEs ([iNews24](https://www.inews24.com/view/1423403)). Cyber-insurance uptake for PII-leak liability is under 10%, with 7,769 policies at end-2024, partly because firms prefer absorbing risk to disclosing incidents ([Maeil Shinmun](https://www.imaeil.com/page/view/2025060111345828161)). The state mirrors the private sector: MSIT's 2026 AI budget of **5.09 trillion won** compares with a hacking prevention and response budget of 79.06 billion won, 1.6% of the AI allocation and down from 5.6% in 2024, and KISA's 9.1 billion won request for a vulnerability-management centre was rejected in full ([Herald Economy](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10877358); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/korea-cuts-funding-for-ai-cyber-center/12906669)). Reported incidents to KISA rose from 1,887 in 2024 to 2,383 in 2025, up 26.3%, and 1,236 in the first half of 2026, up 19.5%, which officials partly attribute to an improved reporting culture ([Financial News](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202604131822482967); [NK Economy](http://www.nkeconomy.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=17071)).

## What has to change, from the server room to the city council

The vendor telemetry that frames the AI threat points at the same door the Korean investigations found. CrowdStrike's 2026 Global Threat Report puts average eCrime breakout time at **29 minutes** and AI-enabled adversary operations up 89% year on year; Mandiant's M-Trends 2026 shows the median time from initial access to handoff to a second group falling from over eight hours in 2022 to **22 seconds** in 2025, with exploits (32%) and voice phishing (11%) the leading vectors; Microsoft reports that over 97% of identity attacks are password spray or brute force and that phishing-resistant MFA blocks more than 99% of unauthorised access; Verizon's DBIR 2026 finds third parties in 48% of breaches, vulnerability exploitation at 31% of initial access, a 43-day median to fix a critical vulnerability and small organisations as 96% of ransomware victims ([CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/2026-crowdstrike-global-threat-report/); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/m-trends-2026-initial-access-handoff-shrinks-from-hours-to-22-seconds/); [Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/asia/2026/01/07/microsoft-releases-2025-digital-defense-report-highlighting-the-changing-cyber-threat-landscape-and-the-importance-of-security-in-the-ai-era/); [Verizon DBIR 2026](https://www.verizon.com/business/resources/infographics/2026-dbir-bis-smb-revenue-losses.pdf)). KISA's own team lead says Korean attacks still exploit externally exposed admin functions, weak authentication and unpatched flaws, and many firms do not know which systems are exposed ([Kyunghyang](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202608062109015)). AI changed speed and scale, not the door. That dictates the order of work below.

### Company level: a hygiene floor before any "AI defence"

For Korean tech companies the first action is the one the banks skipped: an authoritative inventory of every internet-facing system, including the partner portals, loan-agent sites, employee mobile tools, femtocells and temporary servers that sat outside ISMS-P scope and routine checks at Shinhan, KT and SKT, followed by the FSC's own 4 October instruction to close any path to internal data that skips authentication and to block external access except where operationally unavoidable ([FSC release](https://fsc.go.kr/no010101/87869); [Runtime Wire](https://runtimewire.com/article/south-korea-bank-breaches-artex-ai-probe)). Every such portal needs phishing-resistant authentication rather than "simplified" verification, object-level authorisation checks so that cycling identifiers returns nothing, and rate limiting tuned to low-and-slow enumeration, because the Shinhan traffic passed as normal precisely because the agent made few attempts per burst ([The Herald Business](https://mbiz.heraldcorp.com/article/10891096); [Hankyung](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026100427061)). Secrets management is the second pillar: the SKT investigation team and the PIPC's Coupang orders converge on encrypted authentication material in an HSM or KMS, short-lived credentials with automatic rotation, and revocation wired to HR offboarding, which Coupang has since implemented as hardware-based key generation and new monitoring ([Newsis](https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20260117_0003480546); [Digital Daily](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2026061110411439721); [Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20261006001345)). KT's femtocell certificate validity went from ten years to one month by government order; that ratio is a reasonable mental model for any shared credential ([Kukinews](https://www.kukinews.com/article/view/kuk202512290168)). Third, logs: the SKT team recommended at least six months of firewall log retention, EDR, zero trust and quarterly vulnerability checks, and the three criminal referrals for log deletion (KT, LG Uplus, Coupang) make immutable logging and a legal-hold procedure a liability control as much as a forensic one ([Digital Daily](https://m.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2025070412563749518); [SBS](https://news.sbs.co.kr/english/article.do?news_id=N1008682301)). Fourth, penetration testing has to cover the architecture rather than the paths tested, since Coupang's tests never examined gateway-level token validation; KISA's October 2026 drill, which will use an AI agent as the attacker, and the bug-bounty programme that logged 1,837 reports and 99 high-risk findings from 618 white hats in 2025 are both free ways to borrow the attacker's new tooling ([iNews24](https://www.inews24.com/v/mtl/1938043); [MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026090115521166525); [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2025/11/21-533/)). Fifth, report inside the clock regardless of how the fine regime feels: reporting speed is now a direct surcharge factor under PIPA and ex officio investigation removes the option of quiet self-remediation ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/industry/2026/09/29/2026092913220431784); [Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=114618)). For mid-sized firms that cannot staff a SOC against a 29-minute breakout, the realistic path is outsourced detection plus the free shared services (KISA drills, C-TAS, the new attack-surface-management service for small firms opened in August 2026) that uptake data show they are not yet using ([Byline Network](https://byline.network/2026/08/5-444/)).

### Board level: use the new law as a lever, and set a number

The Network Act has handed boards a mechanism they did not have: a statutory CISO with budget-formulation and staffing duties, a committee the CISO chairs, and a duty to report security status to the board. Boards should treat the 1 October duties as a floor, not a filing exercise, because the 78% dual-hatting figure means the title change alone will not change time allocation ([Bizwatch](https://news.bizwatch.co.kr/article/mobile/2025/09/30/0045)). The concrete moves are to put the CISO's reporting line under the CEO with direct board access, as SKT and LG Uplus have done and as the SKT investigation team recommended; to separate CISO and CPO where they are still one person, as KT did in May 2026; to recruit at least one director with security expertise, given the SKT board's zero and Hana Financial's computer-science PhD outside director as the counterexample; and to protect CISO tenure, which the FSC now proposes to guarantee for two years by board resolution in finance ([MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026090716215725377); [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/tech/2026/05/07/2026050708421413504); [Dealsite](https://dealsite.co.kr/articles/153823); [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/finance/2026/10/08/2026100719520811062)). Boards should also set a resourcing benchmark and publish against it: Soonchunhyang's Yeom Heung-yeol argues security budgets should rise gradually to about 10% of IT budgets, the FSS points at the 13% international financial-institution figure, and the National Assembly Research Service recommended mandatory budget ratios for carriers; whichever number a board picks, the execution rate matters as much as the budget, as the banks' three-year decline shows ([Digital Daily](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2026063013030632172); [Kukinews](https://www.kukinews.com/article/view/kuk202505210070); [ETNews](https://www.etnews.com/20260706000007)). A board that wants a reference for what "done" looks like has SKT's first information-security white paper, which tracked 257 tasks with 171 (66.5%) complete by June 2026, though a columnist rightly notes only inputs and process, not outcomes, can be assessed so far ([Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/economy/industry/2026/07/02/20260702031003); [Issue Edico](https://www.issueedico.co.kr/news/article.html?no=38691)). Finally, the board should price the tail: the Coupang fine exceeded the floor of Korean cyber-insurance cover, PIPA already allows up to five times actual damages for intentional or grossly negligent leaks, and under the 2026 PIPA the board itself must now resolve on the CPO appointment ([Insurance Business Asia](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/asia/news/cyber/coupangs-record-fine-dwarfs-koreas-cyber-insurance-floor-584390.aspx); [Kookmin Ilbo](https://www.kmib.co.kr/article/view.asp?arcid=1759995283); [Seoul Shinmun](https://www.seoul.co.kr/news/society/2026/09/10/20260910500300)).

### Sector level: finish the finance bill, share faster, and evaluate what certification buys

Three sector moves follow from the evidence. The first is legislative: the Yoo Dong-soo EFTA bill is the finance-sector twin of the Network Act and has sat in subcommittee since March; the Political Affairs Committee now has five bank CEOs in front of it on 19 October and a reason to move the bill in the November-December session ([Financial News](https://www.fnnews.com/news/202610060551446787); [Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/article/2026100710021437530)). The FSC's own list (twice-yearly assessments across all exposed assets, repeatable enforcement fines, a shorter reporting window, CISO tenure) should ride with it ([Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/finance/2026/10/08/2026100719520811062)). The second is intelligence sharing with after-action learning. The FSS's distribution of 28 attack IPs to about 500 firms within a week is the right reflex, and the Financial Security Institute, which merged the bank and securities ISACs in 2015 because they had not been sharing intrusion data and signed an MOU with FS-ISAC in 2022, is the right body; what it lacks, and FS-ISAC supplies to 16,000 members, is after-action reports with categorised benchmark results, and what Japan's NISC cross-sector exercise supplies with 6,000-plus participants from 14 sectors plus police and defence is a drill that puts telecom, platform and finance in the same room ([Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/law-crime/20261006/28-ip-addresses-behind-recent-hacking-attacks-against-korean-financial-institutions-watchdog); [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2022/11/14-238); [FS-ISAC](https://www.fsisac.com/resilience/exercises); [Dempa Shimbun](https://dempa-digital.com/?p=502932)). KISA's 626-firm drill, which cut the phishing infection rate from 16.8% to 3.7% between halves of 2025, already has the scale to add that benchmarking ([Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=140543)). The third is a tiered baseline for the long tail. ISMS-P is heavyweight, failed to cover KT's femtocells and did not stop any of the five breaches; the UK's Cyber Essentials, which a 2024 DSIT evaluation found 82% of users trust and 53% rely on as their sole external assurance, and Singapore's Cyber Essentials and Cyber Trust marks, backed by 70% co-funded consulting and tied to future government procurement, are models for a lighter mark Korean procurement and insurers could require ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/cyber-essentials-scheme-impact-evaluation/cyber-essentials-impact-evaluation); [CSA](https://www.csa.gov.sg/News-Events/Press-Releases/2022/csa-launches-new-cybersecurity-certification-programme-to-recognise-enterprises-with-good-cybersecurity-practices)). The sector should also fix supplier contracts, since ISMS-P control 2.3.2 already flags outsourcing agreements that carry only NDA boilerplate and 48% of breaches now involve a third party ([IT Wiki](https://itwiki.kr/w/ISMS-P_%EC%9D%B8%EC%A6%9D_%EA%B8%B0%EC%A4%80_2.3.2.%EC%99%B8%EB%B6%80%EC%9E%90_%EA%B3%84%EC%95%BD_%EC%8B%9C_%EB%B3%B4%EC%95%88); [Verizon DBIR 2026](https://www.verizon.com/business/resources/infographics/2026-dbir-bis-smb-revenue-losses.pdf)). The 2027 extension of mandatory disclosure to all roughly 2,700 listed companies gives Korea a comparable spend-and-staff dataset that the UK and Singapore lack; its missing piece, flagged by Korean experts, is an agreed benchmark for "enough" and a published evaluation of whether disclosure or certification changes breach outcomes ([Korea.kr](https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148957777); [Digital Daily](https://www.ddaily.co.kr/page/view/2026070309304466472)).

### Local level: the delivery network exists, demand does not

Korea's regional security infrastructure is real but under-used. MSIT and KISA run 11 regional information-security support centres (Incheon, Daegu, Gwangju, Chungbuk, Busan, Gyeonggi, Ulsan, Gangwon, Gyeongbuk, Chungnam, Jeonnam) offering checks, consulting and solution subsidies to SMEs, with five more planned; regional clusters funded at 2 billion won a year per consortium opened in Busan in 2023 and Chungcheong in 2026; Incheon passed an industrial-security ordinance and won 640 million won in national funding for its six strategic industries; Jeju launched a regional security council in 2024; and the SME Ministry's technology-protection voucher subsidises 80%, 60% or 50% of costs up to 30, 50 or 70 million won by maturity tier ([MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026060915100566223); [Dailian](https://www.dailian.co.kr/news/view/1670720/); [Boan News](https://m.boannews.com/html/detail.html?idx=136796); [eopla](https://eopla.net/magazines/38942)). The C-TAS SME participation rate of 0.03% says these channels have supply without demand. Local governments can create the demand the national programmes cannot: by requiring a baseline mark in municipal and public-agency procurement, by routing voucher money through the regional centres toward the hygiene floor above rather than generic consulting, by running the KISA AI-agent drill for local firms in each cluster, and by using the regional universities that supply the security graduate programmes (eight convergence-security graduate schools produced 325 graduates in six years) to build a local career market, since Ajou's Kwak Jin notes trained people leave or go abroad when there is none ([iNews24](https://www.inews24.com/view/1423403); [E-Today](https://www.etoday.co.kr/news/view/2460937); [Byline Network](https://byline.network/2025/11/7-381/)).

| Level | Highest-leverage action | Evidence it addresses |
|---|---|---|
| Company | Inventory and harden every internet-facing portal (strong auth, object-level authorisation, rate limits); rotate and revoke secrets automatically; keep immutable logs for 6+ months; test the architecture, not the path | Shinhan loan-agent portal; Coupang signing key; KT 10-year cert; three log-deletion referrals |
| Board | Make the 1 Oct 2026 CISO and committee duties real: CEO reporting line, security-qualified director, protected tenure, published spend target near 10% of IT with execution tracked | 78% dual-hatted CISOs; SKT board with zero experts; banks' falling execution rate |
| Sector | Pass the EFTA bill; add after-action benchmarking to FSI/KISA drills; create a tiered SME mark tied to procurement and insurance; fix supplier contracts | Finance bill stalled 10 months; ISMS-P did not prevent any breach; C-TAS SME uptake 0.03% |
| Local | Turn the 11 regional centres, clusters and vouchers into a delivery channel for the hygiene floor, with procurement-driven demand and a local talent market | 8.7% of firms say they need security staff; 35.3% have a security organisation |

## Conclusion

The honest headline for a Korean audience is that the October 2026 attack was cheap, not clever, and that its cheapness is the news. An unattributed operator with a free open-source agent, a budget-model API bought through a reseller and a commercial chatbot for homework reached 68,000 people's data in a week by exploiting weaknesses that the SKT, KT, LG Uplus and Coupang investigations had already named in public. What agentic tooling changed is the economics: the skill, staffing and time required to find and exploit Korea's known weaknesses have collapsed, so every unassessed portal, every shared ten-year certificate and every part-time CISO is now a near-term liability rather than a theoretical one. The GTG-1002 comparison sharpens the policy lesson: this operator never had to jailbreak anything, which means provider-side safeguards will not be the backstop, and defenders should assume the next ARTEX fork runs just as well.

Korea has, unusually, legislated the governance half of the answer before the next incident rather than after it. The test of the next twelve months is whether the Network Act's executive CISO and committee duties change how budgets are set inside companies, whether the Seoul Administrative Court upholds revenue-based fines so the 10% tier means anything, whether the Political Affairs Committee moves the finance bill it has ignored for ten months, and whether the 2027 disclosure expansion is used to publish a benchmark and evaluate outcomes rather than to file more paper. For Korean tech companies specifically, the Coupang accountability gap and the banks' unguarded side doors are one disease: security owned by nobody with authority, measured by certificates rather than exposure, and funded at a ratio that has not moved in three years. The field's reset starts when a board can say what share of IT it spends on security, who reports to it about exposed assets, and how long its longest-lived credential has been alive.
