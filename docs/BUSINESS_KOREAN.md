# 비즈니스 한국어 — translation reference

Luna's working reference for translating English into Korean for Andy. Her charter (`plugins/neural-bridge-core/agents/luna.md`, "Translating English into Korean") sends her here before any work-related translation. Personal or casual translation doesn't need it.

Andy's context: GRC and compliance, UW iSchool faculty, Korean counterparts in both regulatory and academic settings. Most of what he needs translated is email, audit and compliance material, or public writing.

---

## 1. Pick the register first

The single most consequential decision. Getting this wrong makes correct Korean unusable.

| Register | Endings | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| **하십시오체** | -습니다 / -습니까 / -십시오 | External email, clients, regulators, executives, first contact, anything formal or forwardable |
| **해요체** | -해요 / -세요 / -이에요 | Internal messages to familiar colleagues, KakaoTalk work chat, soft follow-ups |
| **한다체 (plain)** | -한다 / -이다 / -했다 | **Documents**: 보고서, 회의록, 정책, 절차서, specs, research writing |

**The trap:** putting a document into 습니다 form. Reports, meeting minutes, policies and procedures are written in plain form in Korean, with no reader being addressed. `본 감사에서 다음과 같은 미흡 사항이 확인되었다.` is right. `확인되었습니다` in a 보고서 reads like a schoolchild's essay. Email is 습니다; the attachment is 한다체.

Mixing registers inside one document is worse than picking the wrong one.

## 2. Email skeleton

**First contact**
```
안녕하십니까, [소속]의 [이름]입니다.

다름이 아니라, [본론].

[요청 사항] 부탁드립니다.

감사합니다.
[이름] 드림
```

**Ongoing thread**
```
안녕하세요, [이름]입니다.

[본론]

감사합니다.
[이름] 드림
```

Notes:
- `다름이 아니라` = "the reason I'm writing." Standard way into the substance after the greeting.
- Sign-off: `드림` is the normal humble form. `올림` is more deferential, for clear superiors or formal institutional letters. `배상` is old-fashioned and mostly for very formal correspondence. Never translate "Best regards" literally.
- Opening pleasantry when the relationship is ongoing: `항상 도움 주셔서 감사합니다`, `늘 신경 써 주셔서 감사합니다`.
- Closing before 감사합니다 when you've asked for something: `번거로우시겠지만 확인 부탁드립니다`.

## 3. Humble forms (겸양어) — your own actions

This is the spine of business Korean. Every verb describing what *you* do goes humble.

| Plain | Humble |
|---|---|
| 말하다 | 말씀드리다 |
| 주다 | 드리다 |
| 보내다 | 보내드리다 |
| 알리다 | 안내드리다 / 공유드리다 |
| 연락하다 | 연락드리다 |
| 요청하다 | 요청드리다 |
| 문의하다 | 문의드리다 |
| 묻다 | 여쭙다 / 여쭤보다 |
| 만나다 | 뵙다 (뵙겠습니다, 뵙고 싶습니다) |
| 보다 (a document) | 검토하다 → 검토드리다 |

## 4. Honorific forms (존경어) — their actions

| Plain | Honorific |
|---|---|
| subject marker 이/가 | 께서 |
| 에게 | 께 |
| 하다 | 하시다 |
| 있다 | 계시다 |
| 먹다 | 드시다 / 잡수시다 |
| 말하다 | 말씀하시다 |
| 이름 | 성함 |
| 나이 | 연세 |
| 집 | 댁 |

`말씀하신 대로` (as you said), `검토해 주신 자료` (the material you reviewed), `보내주신 메일` (the email you sent).

**Never use 당신 for "you."** It is either intimate or hostile, never neutral. Use 직함 (부장님, 팀장님, 교수님), or 님 on the name, or drop the pronoun entirely — Korean drops subjects constantly and the sentence is fine without it.

**씨 is not safe upward.** `김철수 씨` to a superior is a mistake. Use the title.

---

## 5. Cushion phrases (쿠션어)

The skill that separates fluent business Korean from correct business Korean. A bare request reads as an order. Put something in front of it.

- `번거로우시겠지만` — I know it's a bother, but
- `바쁘신 와중에 죄송합니다` — sorry to trouble you when you're busy
- `실례지만` / `죄송하지만`
- `괜찮으시다면` — if it's alright with you
- `가능하시다면` — if possible
- `혹시` — by any chance (softens almost anything, very common)
- `확인 후 회신 주시면 감사하겠습니다`

---

## 6. Requests, ranked soft to firm

| Korean | Feel |
|---|---|
| ~해 주실 수 있을까요? | Softest, question form |
| ~해 주시면 감사하겠습니다 | Soft, very common |
| ~부탁드립니다 | The workhorse, neutral-polite |
| ~해 주시기 바랍니다 | Formal, slightly firm, notice-like |
| ~하시기 바랍니다 | Firm, institutional |

Standing collocations: `검토 부탁드립니다` (please review), `회신 부탁드립니다` (please reply), `확인 부탁드립니다` (please confirm), `참고 부탁드립니다` (FYI), `일정 조율 부탁드립니다` (please coordinate scheduling).

---

## 7. Saying no

Direct refusal is close to unusable in Korean business writing. The forms below all mean no; the reader knows.

- `어렵습니다` — "it's difficult" = no
- `어려울 것 같습니다` — softer no
- `검토가 필요합니다` — not now
- `내부적으로 논의해 보겠습니다` — probably no
- `조율이 필요할 것 같습니다` — needs negotiation
- `현재로서는 어렵습니다` — no for now, door open

**Never** `안 됩니다` or `못 합니다` to a client or external party.

When Andy's English says a flat no and you render it this way, tell him. He should know his message got softer.

---

## 8. Time and scheduling

- 금일 (today) · 익일 (next day) · 금주 (this week) · 차주 (next week) · 익월 (next month) — formal written forms
- `~까지 회신 주시면 감사하겠습니다` — by [date]
- `가능하신 시간 알려주시면 조율하겠습니다` — send your availability and I'll work around it
- `일정 조율` — schedule coordination
- `오전 10시` / `오후 3시` — 24-hour clock is uncommon in Korean business writing
- `빠른 시일 내에` / `최대한 빨리` — ASAP. Never write 아삽.

## 9. GRC and compliance vocabulary

Andy's domain. Getting these wrong is expensive, so if you're unsure, flag it instead of guessing.

| English | Korean |
|---|---|
| compliance | 규정 준수 / 컴플라이언스 |
| internal controls | 내부 통제 |
| risk management | 위험 관리 / 리스크 관리 |
| audit | 감사 |
| internal audit | 내부 감사 |
| audit response | 감사 대응 |
| findings / deficiencies | 지적 사항 / 미흡 사항 |
| corrective action | 시정 조치 |
| evidence, audit artifacts | 증적 / 증빙 |
| control item | 통제 항목 |
| due diligence | 실사 |
| regulator | 규제 기관 |
| data privacy | 개인정보 보호 |
| information security | 정보보안 |
| policy / guideline / procedure | 정책 / 지침 / 절차 |
| implementation | 이행 |
| scope | 범위 |
| remediation plan | 개선 계획 |
| governance | 거버넌스 |

Korean institutions and frameworks worth knowing by name:
- **개인정보보호법 (PIPA)** — Korea's data protection act
- **개인정보보호위원회 (PIPC)** — the privacy regulator
- **금융감독원 (FSS)** — Financial Supervisory Service
- **ISMS-P** — Korea's combined infosec and privacy certification, the one Korean counterparts will actually reference
- **전자금융감독규정** — electronic financial supervision regulations

Framework names stay in English or in their established Korean form: GDPR, DORA, NIST, ISO 27001. Don't invent Korean translations for these.

Company reference: `귀사` = your company (formal), `저희` = our company (humble). `우리 회사` is fine internally, not to a client.

## 10. Traps that make Korean read as translated

- **Subject overuse.** `저는` in every sentence is the loudest tell. Korean drops subjects. Drop them.
- **"Please" ≠ 제발.** 제발 is pleading. Use 부탁드립니다 or ~해 주세요.
- **"I think"** → often better as `~것 같습니다` than `생각합니다`. Softer and more natural.
- **"Let me know"** → `알려주시면 감사하겠습니다`, not `알려줘`.
- **"Following up"** → `지난번에 말씀드린 건 관련하여` or `재차 문의드립니다`.
- **"Best regards"** → doesn't translate. It's `감사합니다` + `드림`.
- **Pronouns generally.** English is pronoun-heavy, Korean is not. He/she/it/they usually vanish.
- **Conjunction density.** English business prose chains clauses; Korean prefers shorter sentences. Break them up.
- **Passive voice.** English compliance writing loves it. Korean tolerates 되다 forms in documents but they pile up fast. Prefer active where it doesn't change meaning.
- **Emphasis by capitals or bold** doesn't carry. Restructure the sentence instead.

## 11. What to hand Andy

Default output:

1. The Korean.
2. One line naming the register and why (`하십시오체, external and first contact`).
3. A note only if you changed the force of something — softened a refusal, added a cushion, split a sentence.

When the register genuinely can't be inferred, give both labelled versions rather than asking. Ask about the recipient only when seniority or internal-vs-external actually changes the output and the thread doesn't tell you.

For anything going to a regulator, an auditor, or a client, say so if a term is one you're not confident in. A hedge costs him nothing; a wrong 감사 term in an audit response costs him a lot.
